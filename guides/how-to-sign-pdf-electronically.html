This tutorial explains what an in-browser signature actually is, what it isn't, and how to add one to any PDF document with zero server uploads.

What Is an Electronic Signature?

An electronic signature (e-signature) is a digital representation of a person's intent to sign a document — it can be a drawn signature image, a typed name, or a cryptographically certified signature from a dedicated signing platform. Laws such as the United States ESIGN Act and the European Union eIDAS Regulation generally treat electronic signatures as capable of carrying the same legal weight as pen-and-paper ones. But whether a particular signature actually holds up depends on the circumstances — the parties' intent and consent, what was agreed, and how the signed document is kept — not on which tool created the mark. A drawn or typed signature, the kind this guide covers, is one valid form; it does not carry the identity verification, certificate, or audit trail that dedicated e-signature platforms add for higher-stakes agreements.

3 Ways to Create Your E-Signature

Depending on your device and preference, there are three primary methods to sign a document digitally:

  • Draw Signature (Freehand): Use your mouse cursor, touchpad, stylus, or fingertip on touchscreens (tablets and smartphones) to create a natural handwritten signature.
  • Type Signature (Cursive Typography): Enter your name to generate a cursive script signature.
  • Upload Image Stamp: Upload a pre-existing transparent PNG scan or high-contrast photo of your physical handwritten signature.
Security Best Practice: Never upload confidential contracts containing bank account numbers, tax IDs, or trade secrets to unknown cloud conversion websites. Always verify that signing occurs locally on your machine.
